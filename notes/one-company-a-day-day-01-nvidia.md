For the next 30 days, I’m using my own product to investigate one company a day and share something useful that the earnings headline missed.

Today: NVIDIA.

Everyone knows revenue is exploding. The latest 10-Q contains the more interesting story.

$NVDA reported $81.6B of quarterly revenue, up 85% year over year. But three direct customers produced 21%, 17% and 16% of revenue—54% combined. A year earlier, its two largest disclosed direct customers represented 30% combined.

That means the AI boom is producing extraordinary growth while NVIDIA’s disclosed customer concentration is also rising sharply.

The second thing I noticed was earnings quality.

Net income reached $58.3B, but $15.9B came from gains on equity securities. Operating cash flow was $50.3B. NVIDIA’s underlying business still produced enormous cash, but almost 27% of reported net income came from an investment-gain line that can move in either direction.

Then I followed where the cash went:

• $18.6B invested in private companies and infrastructure funds
• $19.3B paid toward share repurchases
• $1.76B spent on property, equipment and intangibles
• $32.4B of future leases disclosed, mainly for data centers supporting R&D

Put those pieces together and the question changes.

It isn’t simply whether AI demand keeps growing. It is whether a handful of major customers keep funding huge infrastructure programs—and whether NVIDIA’s expanding portfolio of ecosystem investments continues adding value rather than earnings volatility.
