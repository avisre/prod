## Product story

**e.g. The tool I wished existed when I ran my **

> I built the research workspace I wished existed - grounded for stocks and fund-aware

****

> I built StockPortfolio.pro because I got burned by AI. I'd ask a chatbot for a company's free cash flow or margins, paste the number into a discussion, and someone would reply with the actual 10-K showing it was wrong. The AI had simply made it up - plausible-sounding fiction. For trivia that's annoying; for money, it's unforgivable.  So I flipped the architecture. Stock answers are built from reported numbers in SEC 10-K and 10-Q filings instead of model memory. ETFs and mutual funds use a different, fund-specific framework covering costs, holdings, allocation, performance, and risk. Company metrics are never misapplied to fund positions.  Around that core I built company deep dives, screeners, side-by-side comparisons, fund rankings, and one portfolio for stocks, ETFs, and mutual funds. Investors can ask detailed questions, generate research reports, and share AI answers with platform-ready copy for X, Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook, WhatsApp, and Reddit. It's a solo project built to give regular investors research they can inspect and use without paying institutional prices.

## FAQs

**FAQ 1 question**

> What is a Research Dossier?

**FAQ 1 answer**

> The full write-up on a covered US-listed company: decision brief, filed trajectory, segments, valuation including a reverse-DCF, bull and bear case, and risks. Every company gets the same twelve sections in the same order, so two or three line up side by side. Every figure carries its fiscal period and a link to the SEC filing it came from, and no prompting is required — you type the ticker.

**FAQ 2 question**

> What does the Filing Change Monitor do?

**FAQ 2 answer**

> When a company files a new 10-K, 10-Q or 8-K, it is read against the previous filing of the same form. You get the risk-factor edits, the guidance and outlook language, and the numbers that moved — quoted verbatim with an EDGAR link, ranked by materiality. Starter watches 1 company, Investor 4, and Pro 8.

**FAQ 3 question**

> How do the AI credits work?

**FAQ 3 answer**

> Every tier includes every feature; tiers differ by how many reports you run each month. A Research Dossier costs 10 credits, a Filing Monitor report 5, a comparison 5, and a follow-up question 2. Starter's 100 credits is 10 dossiers or 20 filing reports a month; Investor gets 300 and Pro 800. Credits reset on the 1st.

**FAQ 4 question**

> Where does the data come from?

**FAQ 4 answer**

> Company financials come from SEC EDGAR 10-K and 10-Q filings. ETF and mutual-fund research uses fund market data for costs, holdings, allocation, performance, and risk. The product keeps the two data models separate.

**FAQ 5 question**

> Is this investment advice?

**FAQ 5 answer**

> No. It surfaces real reported numbers and lets you analyze them; it doesn't tell you what to buy. Always do your own due diligence.

**FAQ 6 question**

> How is the lifetime deal different from your regular pricing?

**FAQ 6 answer**

> Normally this is a subscription. With this deal you pay once and keep it forever — no renewals. The tiers differ by monthly AI credits: 100 (Starter), 300 (Investor), 800 (Pro), and by how many companies the Filing Change Monitor watches: 1, 4 or 8. Everything else — Research Dossiers, deep-dive pages, screeners, comparison, portfolio tracking and up to 19 years of filed statements — is included on all three tiers.

**FAQ 7 question**

> How is this different from asking ChatGPT about an investment?

**FAQ 7 answer**

> General AI tools may answer from memory. StockPortfolio.pro retrieves company financials from SEC filings and fund facts from fund market data, applies the correct analysis for each asset type, and shows when the available source does not contain an answer.

**FAQ 8 question**

> Does the AI ever make up numbers?

**FAQ 8 answer**

> It is designed not to. Company financial figures are retrieved from SEC filings instead of model memory, and fund answers use retrieved fund market data. If the relevant source does not contain an answer, the assistant should say so rather than invent a figure.

**FAQ 9 question**

> Which assets are covered?

**FAQ 9 answer**

> There are deep-dive pages for 6,000+ US-listed stocks. ETFs and mutual funds can be researched by ticker using fund-specific costs, holdings, allocation, performance, and risk. Fund rankings cover eligible ETF and mutual-fund screening universes.
