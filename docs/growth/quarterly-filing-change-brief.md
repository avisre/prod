# Quarterly filing-change brief — one-pager template

A one-page brief a human sends manually, once a quarter, to a named person about
names they actually hold or follow. **No code, no automation, no list send.**
This is a template plus the rules for using it — not a campaign.

## What this is for

Filing season is the one moment when someone who follows a handful of US
companies has a concrete, dated problem: several filings landed and they have to
read them. A short brief that has already done the first pass on *their* names,
sent in that window, is useful on its own whether or not they ever buy anything.

That is the whole bet. It only works if it is genuinely useful when ignored.

## Rules

1. **Send to one named person at a time.** If it could go to a list unchanged, it
   is not this. Rewrite it or don't send it.
2. **Their names, not ours.** Pick the 3–5 companies that person actually holds
   or writes about. A brief on names they don't follow is spam with formatting.
3. **Every figure carries its filing link.** No exceptions. The link is the
   product; a brief with an unsourced number defeats its own point.
4. **No estimates and no price talk.** What changed in the document, and what it
   means mechanically. Not what the stock will do.
5. **One ask, at the bottom, small.** Or no ask at all for the first send.
6. **If nothing material changed, say that.** "Three of your five filed and
   nothing moved" is a real, useful finding and it buys more credibility than a
   manufactured insight.

## Timing

Send in the two weeks after the bulk of a quarter's 10-Qs land, while the reader
still has the filings open. Late is worse than short.

---

## Template

> **Subject:** Q[N] filing changes — [NAME 1], [NAME 2], [NAME 3]
>
> [First name] —
>
> [One sentence on why these names and why now. E.g. "You've written about
> [NAME 1] twice this year, so here's what changed in its Q[N] filings and two
> others you follow."]
>
> ---
>
> **[COMPANY] ([TICKER]) — [FILING TYPE], filed [DATE]**
>
> *What changed:* [1–2 sentences. The specific edit or movement, not a summary of
> the company.]
>
> *The numbers:* [Figure, prior-period figure, the delta. State the fiscal period
> for both — if the periods differ, say so rather than comparing them anyway.]
>
> *Why it might matter:* [1–2 sentences, mechanical. What it does to margins,
> cash, share count, covenant headroom. Stop before the investment conclusion.]
>
> *Source:* [direct sec.gov link to the filed document]
>
> ---
>
> *(Repeat the block for each company. Three to five. If a name filed and nothing
> material changed, keep its block and say exactly that in one line — it is a
> finding.)*
>
> ---
>
> **What I didn't check**
>
> [Be explicit about the gaps. E.g. "I didn't read the full risk-factor diff on
> [NAME], only the financial statements." A named gap is worth more than an
> implied claim of completeness.]
>
> ---
>
> [Optional, one line, no pressure. E.g. "This is the first pass our Filing
> Change Monitor does automatically — happy to point it at the rest of your
> list if that's useful." Or omit entirely on a first send.]
>
> — [Your name]

## After sending

Log it in `marketing/targeted-acquisition/OUTREACH-QUEUE.md` like any other
outreach: who, when, which names, and whether they replied. If a brief is worth
sending it is worth tracking, and if it isn't tracked it will be sent twice.

## What would make this worth repeating

A reply that engages with a specific finding. Not a thank-you, not a click — an
argument about one of the numbers. That is the signal that the brief was read as
research rather than as marketing. If two consecutive quarters produce none,
stop sending and reconsider the format rather than the list.
