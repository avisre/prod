## The product-market-fit signal

The narrow signal is not “people want more finance tools.” It is:

> A serious stock researcher asks a concrete company question, gets a fast answer and can open the filing evidence without reconstructing the source trail manually.

Evidence:

- six of seven redeemed buyers used Ask;
- all Tier 2/3 buyers used Ask;
- one Tier 3 user returned across five AI-use days and made 54 calls;
- the review calls the product efficient but highlights the need to verify accuracy;
- presale/Q&A conversations ask about reliability, source/export continuity and monitoring.

The retention extension is “know what changed,” because filing/portfolio monitoring supplies a reason to return. This is less crowded than generic “AI stock research,” while staying within the product’s SEC-source advantage.

## Why visitors are not buying

The audit cannot assign a single reason to 120 recent AppSumo nonbuyers. The measured evidence supports four hypotheses to test in order:

1. Trust: visitors need to see the source and limits, not absolute claims.
2. Comprehension: the full question → answer → evidence workflow is not immediately obvious.
3. Tier clarity: repeated public questions ask how AppSumo and website plans map.
4. Feature boundary: ETF, export, alerts and API/MCP scope must be explicit.

Do not “solve” this with more features until a controlled listing/demo test isolates one issue.

## Adversarial review

This recommendation may fail because the paid sample is tiny; AppSumo LTD buyers differ from subscribers; attribution is missing for eight buyers; one heavy Ask user distorts usage; the partner-link economics are untested; X clicks may have no buying intent; professional cohorts may not buy; Clarity is observational; search impressions are not commercial intent; and competitor adoption does not prove willingness to pay for StockPortfolio.

The report therefore proposes hard gates: validate the first partner-link order, require orders rather than clicks, make no major build, and kill Desk/API work without priced demand.
