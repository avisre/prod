01What it is

Apple sells hardware at a premium and then keeps selling to the people who bought it.

The iPhone is still the centre of the business, but the money that grows fastest is the money that comes after the sale: the App Store, iCloud, Music, advertising and the rest of the services attached to more than two billion active devices. Hardware is a low-margin, cyclical business. Services is a high-margin, repeating one, and it is where the company's profit growth has come from for five years.

Source: Form 10-K FY2024, Item 1 and Item 8.

02How the money moves

Customers paid $391B. Making and selling it cost $210B, and $93.7B ended up as profit.

That is the whole story in one line: of every dollar a customer spends, 54¢ goes to the cost of what Apple sells, 22¢ to running the company, interest and tax, and 24¢ is kept. The 54¢ is the part worth watching — it has been shrinking for five years, from 62¢ in FY2019, which is a sign of real pricing power in the hardware.

The pressure is below that line. Research and development rose to $31.4B and selling and administration to $26.1B, and a tax provision of $29.7B took a larger share of profit than the year before. That combination is why a company with a rising gross margin still reported lower net income.

Source: Form 10-K FY2024, Item 8 — consolidated statements of operations.

03What it sells

Over half of all sales are still one product: the iPhone.

What this means: concentration cuts both ways. A weak iPhone year is a weak Apple year — but Services is the highest-margin part of the business, and it keeps growing.

05What's working

Services keeps compounding, and it is the most profitable thing Apple sells.

  • ↑Services revenue has grown every year and now carries a quarter of all sales at a much higher margin than hardware.
  • ↑Buybacks shrink the share count year after year, so earnings per share rise even when net income does not.
  • ↑Cash and marketable securities still exceed total debt — the dividend is not a stretch.
06What could go wrong

The iPhone is half the business, and its growth has stalled.

  • ↓iPhone revenue fell last year. A product that is half of sales cannot stall for long without the whole company flatlining.
  • ↓Regulators in the US and EU are attacking the App Store commission, which is the heart of the Services margin.
  • ↓Manufacturing and final assembly remain concentrated in a handful of countries, mostly in Asia.
07Risks

Three things could break the story. One of them is serious.

China exposureSerious

China is both a major market and the centre of assembly. A ban on either side is a direct hit to revenue.

App Store regulationWorth watching

Forced alternative payments would trim the commission on the highest-margin revenue Apple has.

Supply-chain concentrationLow

Assembly is diversifying, but slowly, and the 10-K added a new clause about it this year.
