How it makes money

Customers paid $391B. Cost of making it took $210B, and $93.7B ended up as profit.

What this means: a 46% gross margin is a genuinely strong business — the pressure is in what it spends below the line and in a tax bill that rose with overseas profits.

Over half of all sales are still one product: the iPhone.

Is it growing?

Sales are up 8.5% a year over five years — but the last two have been flat.

What this means: this is a mature compounder, not a fast grower. The five-year average flatters it — the honest read is that growth has stalled at the top line.

Where the money goes

Of every $1 a customer spends, Apple keeps 24 cents.

Cost of what it sells 54¢ Opex, tax & interest 22¢ Kept as profit 24¢
Gross margin per $1 · FY2019–FY202446.2¢

What this means: the 54¢ cost of what it sells has been shrinking for five years — 24¢ retained per dollar of sales is well above the average large US company, and it is what funds the buyback and the dividend.

What’s working

Services keeps compounding, and it is the most profitable thing Apple sells.

  • Services revenue has grown every year and now carries a quarter of all sales at a much higher margin than hardware.
  • Buybacks shrink the share count year after year, so earnings per share rise even when net income does not.
  • Cash and marketable securities still exceed total debt — the dividend is not a stretch.
What could go wrong

The iPhone is half the business, and its growth has stalled.

  • iPhone revenue fell last year. A product that is half of sales cannot stall for long without the whole company flatlining.
  • Regulators in the US and EU are attacking the App Store commission, which is the heart of the Services margin.
  • Manufacturing and final assembly remain concentrated in a handful of countries, mostly in Asia.
Risks

Three things could break the story. One of them is serious.

China exposureSerious

China is both a major market and the centre of assembly. A ban on either side is a direct hit to revenue.

App Store regulationWorth watching

Forced alternative payments would trim the commission on the highest-margin revenue Apple has.

Supply-chain concentrationWorth watching

Assembly is diversifying, but slowly, and the 10-K added a new clause about it this year.

The case in two minutes

A 46% gross margin and a stalled top line, priced as though growth returns.

Apple still earns more per dollar of sales than almost any large manufacturer, and Services keeps widening the mix toward higher margin. But the last two fiscal years have been flat, the iPhone is half of revenue, and the multiple already discounts growth the company has not delivered since 2022.

The bull case rests on Services and the installed base. The bear case is that both are exposed to the same regulator, and that a flat top line makes the buyback the only remaining lever on earnings per share.

Financial trajectory

Six fiscal years of filed statements, and what they show.

  • Sales grew 8.5% a year for five years, but fell 2.8% in FY2023 and recovered only 2.0% in FY2024 — the growth is not linear and has stalled.
  • Gross margin improved 0.7pt to 46.2%, so the cost of making things is not the problem; the pressure sits in operating expense and tax.
  • Operating margin fell 3.1pt to 31.5% as R&D stepped up — a deliberate trade of near-term margin for product pipeline.
  • Free cash flow has been positive in all six years and covers the dividend more than four times over.
